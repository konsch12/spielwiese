Definitions:

Closed population = population without migration flows, only births and deaths

Open population = affected by births, deaths, immigration and emigration

Population change = natural increase + net migration

Net migration = immigration - emigration

Migration in Demography:

  • change in size of population

  • migrants may have different mortality and fertility rates

  • different characteristics: age structure, sex ratio, education

Definitions of Migration

A universally accepted definition of a migrant does not exist at international level. (country of birth, citizenship, past addresses, etc.)

Definition of Migration according to IOM:

Migration is the movement of persons away from their place of usual residence, either across an international border or within a state, to a new residence.

UN Definition of usual place of residence

The place at which the person lives at the time of the census and has been there for some time and intends to stay there for some time.

UN recommends a threshold of 12 months when considering place of usual residence.

Definition of migration (Yusuf et al. 2014)

Spatial movement of a person which requires:

  • a change in place

  • involves crossing political/administrative border

Theories of international Migration

  • no single coherent theory of international migration

  • early theories are from the study of internal migration

  • focus on interconnectedness of the levels of analysis

  • some theories address some types of migration

  • most theories are more applicable to explain voluntary migration

  • recent focus on individual agency and meso level theories -> connect individual actors within social networks

  • migration aspirations vs. their translation into behaviour

  • three main levels of analysis

    • macro level: insititutional factor

    • meso-level networks, communities

    • micro level indivfoufal, household

Ravenstein’s laws of Migration (1885)

explain dynamics of human movements across space

  • Migrants move only a short distance

  • process of absorption, whereby people immediately surrounding a rapidly growing town move into and the gaps they leave are filled by migrants from more distant areas, continues until attractive force (pull factors) are spent.

  • process of dispersion

  • each migration flow produces a compensating counter flow

  • long-distance migrants go to one of the great centres of commerce and industry

  • natives of towns are less migratory than those from rural areas

  • females are more migratory than males

  • economic factors are the main cause of migration

Step-wise process of migration

  • migration happens step by step, gradual process

  • often first move internally to urban areas

Push factors:

poverty, environmental factors, conflict, economic uncertainty, market conditions, politically unsafe, lack of social care policies (childcare, medical system), education

Pull factors:

employment, education, family reunification, weather/climate preferences, political stability, land availability (housing), culture/lifestyle, healthcare, dating opportunities

-> often inverse of another

-> often combination of factors lead to actual move

Refugees

fundamental human right to cross international borders to seek protection from violence and persecution

Refugees: people fleeing violence and oppression

Definitions

refugees = person who owing to a well-founded fear or being persecuted for reasons of race, religion, nationality, membership of a particular social group or political opinion, is outside the country of his nationality and is unable to or owing to such fear is unwilling to avail himself of the protection of that country. (UN 1951)

asylum seekers = person who applied for a refugees status and is still awaiting decision on her/his recognition as refugee (or other decision such as temporary or subsidiary protection - cannot bring family members)

Is the number of refugees increasing?

in 2020 11% of global migrant stock were migrants.

Countries hosting refugees

  • majority of refugees are hosted in low and lower middle-income countries

  • most refugees seek protection in countries close to their country of origin (e.g. 91% Afghan refugees are in neighboring countries)

  • many refugees remain in exile for protracted periods of time
